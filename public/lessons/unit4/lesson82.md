## Expressing Surprise: ~구나

~구나 is added when the speaker realizes or notices something for the first time. It is informal — used when talking to oneself or to close friends. It attaches directly to the stems of adjectives and 이다, and to verbs in the form ~는구나 (present) or ~았/었구나 (past).

### With Adjectives

- **아, 정말 예쁘구나!** = Oh, it's really pretty!
- **방이 많이 깨끗해졌구나** = Oh, the room got really clean
- **오늘 날씨가 좋구나!** = Oh, the weather is nice today!

### With Verbs: ~는구나 / ~았/었구나

- **지금 밥을 먹는구나!** = Oh, you're eating now!
- **벌써 숙제를 끝냈구나** = Oh, you already finished your homework
- **매일 아침에 운동을 하는구나** = Oh, (I realize) you exercise every morning

### With 이다

- **그 사람이 의사였구나** = Oh, that person was a doctor
- **오늘이 결혼 기념일이구나!** = Oh, today is your wedding anniversary!

### Polite Forms: ~군요 (and the spoken variant ~구나요)

The standard polite form is ~군요. The variant ~구나요 is also heard in casual polite speech; both express the same newly-noticed realization.

- **아이가 많이 자랐군요** = Oh, your child has grown up a lot
- **시간이 참 빠르군요** = My, how time flies
- **한국어를 정말 잘하시군요!** = Oh my, you really do speak Korean well!

### ~겠구나

When guessing about a situation after noticing something:

- **아프겠구나** = Oh, that must hurt
- **돈이 많이 들었겠구나** = Oh, that must have cost a lot of money
